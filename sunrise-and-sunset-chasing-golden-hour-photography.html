Sunrise and Sunset Chasing: Golden Hour Photography

Capture softer natural light and dramatic outdoor scenes with simple ideas for planning sunrise and sunset photography.

5 min read • Updated for 2026

"The best light can transform an ordinary landscape, but being in the right place at the right time matters just as much as the camera."

Why Golden Hour Is So Photogenic

The periods around sunrise and sunset can create softer, warmer light that changes rapidly across the landscape. Long shadows, glowing skies, and changing colours can add depth and atmosphere to photographs.

You do not need professional equipment to make the most of these moments. A phone camera can produce beautiful results when you pay attention to timing, composition, and the direction of the light.

1. Scout Your Location Before the Light Changes

Arrive early enough to understand the surroundings. Look for interesting foreground elements, open views, reflections, paths, trees, buildings, or natural shapes that could frame the scene.

2. Check the Sunrise or Sunset Time

The strongest colours may appear before sunrise or after the sun has technically disappeared below the horizon. Check the expected timing and give yourself enough time to arrive, set up, and find a comfortable viewpoint.

3. Use the Landscape as Your Composition

Instead of pointing directly at the brightest part of the sky every time, look for ways to include the surrounding landscape. A path leading toward the horizon, a tree in silhouette, or a reflective surface can give the photograph a stronger sense of place.

4. Experiment With Silhouettes

When the sky is significantly brighter than the foreground, try exposing for the sky to create a silhouette. Trees, mountains, people, and architectural shapes can become strong visual elements against colourful light.

5. Keep the Camera Steady

Lower light can make camera shake more noticeable. Hold your phone or camera securely and use a stable surface or tripod when appropriate. A steady frame can help preserve detail as the light becomes softer.

6. Photograph the Changing Light

Do not stop after taking one photograph. The scene can change dramatically within minutes. Try different angles and compositions as the sun moves, clouds shift, and the colours develop.

7. Protect the Natural Setting

Getting the perfect photograph should never require damaging the landscape. Stay on suitable paths, respect restricted areas, and avoid disturbing wildlife or fragile vegetation while moving into position.

Final Thoughts

Golden hour photography is as much about patience as equipment. Arriving early, understanding the landscape, and staying long enough to watch the light change can produce more interesting images.

Keep experimenting with silhouettes, reflections, foreground details, and different viewpoints, and let the changing light guide your composition.
