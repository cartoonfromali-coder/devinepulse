Chewy Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookie Recipe

Brown butter gives classic chocolate chip cookies a deeper, nutty flavour while a careful balance of sugar and flour helps create chewy centres and lightly crisp edges. This simple technique can turn an everyday cookie recipe into something much more flavourful.

1 Brown the Butter Carefully

Melt butter in a light-coloured saucepan so you can easily see its progress. Continue heating until the milk solids turn golden brown and the butter develops a toasted, nutty aroma. Remove it from the heat before the solids become too dark.

2 Let the Butter Cool

Warm brown butter should cool before it is mixed with the other ingredients. Adding it while extremely hot can affect the texture of the dough and partially cook the eggs. Let it become warm rather than piping hot before continuing.

3 Balance the Sugars

Brown sugar contributes moisture and chewiness, while granulated sugar encourages spreading and crispness around the edges. Using both creates a more interesting texture than relying on one type of sugar alone.

4 Choose Good Chocolate

Chocolate chips are convenient, but chopped chocolate can create larger melted pockets throughout the cookie. A combination of chips and chopped chocolate gives you both defined pieces and rich melted areas.

5 Chill Before Baking

Chilling the dough allows the flour to hydrate and helps the cookies hold their shape in the oven. Even a short rest can improve the final texture, while a longer chill can deepen the flavour.

6 Do Not Overbake

Remove the cookies when the edges are set and lightly coloured but the centres still look slightly soft. They will continue cooking on the hot baking tray after leaving the oven, helping preserve that chewy texture.

Conclusion

Brown butter adds a remarkable amount of flavour to chocolate chip cookies with very little extra effort. Combine it with balanced sugars, quality chocolate, chilled dough, and careful baking for cookies with crisp edges, chewy centres, and a rich toasted aroma.
